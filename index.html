Aimbot

Fully autonomous turret.

Aimbot finds, tracks, and destroys drone threats on its own, with or without an operator in the loop. Unlike other systems, targeting and acquisition are built into a single, highly portable unit.

Transportable by a single person. Quickly mounted to any vehicle or equipment for an instant cUAS solution. Plug it into existing C2 systems, or let it run standalone.

Test targets

The drones we shoot.

We don't test against cheap, plastic drones off a store shelf. In modern combat, drones are purpose-built for strike missions. These drones are extremely tough to kill, as their carbon fiber frames shrug off all but the most powerful shotgun ammunition.

Therefore, we test against the same kind of drones actually seen in modern combat. This practice gets at our core philosophy: test with real hardware in realistic environments. Sometimes, though, just for fun, we strap an RPG shell to the bottom for some fireworks.

Proprietary AI

We train our own models.

Every Theron system runs on models we train in house, on real data we collect in the field. They learn to tell non-threats, such as birds or distant aircraft, from imminent UAS threats. Every range day makes the next engagement faster and more accurate.

Detection, acquisition, tracking, and fire control all run on the device itself. No cloud connection required.

Built in house

NDAA compliant hardware.

We are developing our own NDAA compliant hardware in house, from actuators to sensors. One of our goals as a company is to put America back on track as a producer of critical hardware, not just a buyer of it.

The designs use materials and components made domestically, so a conflict cannot cut off the parts we need. Building these systems ourselves keeps the supply chain short, the iteration cycle fast, and every part under our control. Our component designs are also intended to be dual use, meaning they have both military and civilian applications.
